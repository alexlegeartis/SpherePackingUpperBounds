# Exact 10-block E8 code-neighbor lattice in dimension80

## Result and scope

An explicit even unimodular rank80 lattice Λ of minimum8 is reconstructed from Bachoc–Nebe's code-neighbor construction. The integral Gram, exact block coordinate map, finite quadratic complement, and16 diagonal-shift representatives are supplied. This is **not** a claim that this chosen neighbor is isometric to the particular catalogue lattice L80. No covering-radius, depth6, projected-packing gain, or new record is asserted.

Primary source: C. Bachoc and G. Nebe, *Extremal lattices of minimum8*, https://www.math.rwth-aachen.de/~Gabriele.Nebe/papers/extremm22.pdf . Relevant rendered printed pages9–14 were read directly: Proposition4.1, Lemmas4.3/4.4, Propositions4.5/4.6 and Theorem4.7. The lower minimum proof is explicitly inherited from Theorem4.7 after verifying its construction hypotheses. This package is producer-verified, not yet independently audited.

## Coordinate conventions

`lattice80.npz` is the main exact dataset; `gram_and_block_map.json` supplies the principal arrays in a plain-text alternative. All arrays are integers.

Let E be `e8_twice_euclidean_basis`. Its rows divided by2 form the E8 basis selected by the decoder. Let G=EEᵀ/4=`e8_gram`. A row x of80 root coefficients represents ten E8 blocks and has squared norm x diag(G,...,G)xᵀ/2 in this construction.

Let M=`twice_root_coordinate_basis`. Rows of M/2 are a Z-basis of Λ in those block-root coordinates. Thus the stored80×80 Gram is

    Gram(Λ)=M diag(G,...,G) Mᵀ/8.

Equivalently, a physical Euclidean basis is M diag(E,...,E)/(4√2). The Gram is integral, even, positive definite, and has determinant1. Positivity follows from the nonsingular Euclidean coordinate basis, not a floating-point eigenvalue test.

## Polarization and code

The rows T1,T2 are complementary four-dimensional totally singular subspaces of E8/2E8 for q(x)=xGxᵀ/2 mod2. Their cross-pairing is the identity. The lattices L_i are generated by2I and T_i in E8 root coordinates. Both have index16 in E8. Each `L1_isometry.json` and `L2_isometry.json` gives an explicit unimodular basis change S such that

    S Gram(L_i) Sᵀ =2 G_standard_E8.

This directly verifies L_i≅√2E8; no uniqueness-classification assumption is needed.

C is precisely the binary5×10 generator printed in Lemma4.4. Its dual is generated by[Pᵀ|I] when C=[I|P]. Exact enumeration verifies both have distribution1+15z^4+15z^6+z^10 and intersection{0,1}. The residue code is T1⊗C + T2⊗Cperp, of binary dimension40. Its preimage LC in E8^10, with the half-scaled metric above, has determinant1. `LC_root_basis` is an exact row basis.

## Neighbor and quadratic data

Define L'={x∈LC:Σx_i∈2L1}. Its exact row basis is `Lprime_root_basis`; its index in LC is16, checked by a rank4 binary constraint and exact determinants. Define L''=LC+{(z/2,...,z/2):z∈L1}. `Ldoubleprime_twice_root_basis` represents its basis multiplied by2; its index over LC is16.

The sum map ψ identifies L''/L' with L1/2L1. In L1 basis coordinates its quadratic form is q(v)=v Gram(L1)vᵀ/4 mod2, with polar form Gram(L1)/2 mod2. The stored old quotient basis Aq represents2E8/2L1, the image of LC/L'. The stored new quotient basis Bq is a complementary totally singular four-space. Exhaustive16-element spans verify singularity, and the combined eight rows span all256 quotient elements. The exact basis-level sum-map quadratic and polar identities are also checked.

Set Z=Bq F, where F is the L1 root-coordinate basis. These four rows are `diagonal_lifts`. The constructed lattice is

    Λ=L' + Σ_j Z·(Z_j/2,...,Z_j/2).

Its index over L' is16. It is exactly the inverse image of Bq in L'' and therefore satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem4.7. That theorem gives minimum at least8. `minimum8_coefficients` is an explicit lattice vector of norm8, obtained from root-coordinate blocks(2e_1,−2e_1,0,...,0), so the minimum equals8.

## Decoder shifts

The16 binary sums z of the four rows Z are distinct modulo2E8 and represent L1/2E8. The16 neighbors can be written L'+(z/2,...,z/2). Twice each half-diagonal shift belongs L': diagonal(z) lies LC because the all-ones binary codeword belongs C, and its sum10z belongs2L1. Also5z≡z mod2L1 lies Bq. Thus the proposed decoder correction κ(z) is identically zero for these exact representatives. Carry constraints from the LC representatives still have to be enforced; no closest-point absence result is claimed here.
