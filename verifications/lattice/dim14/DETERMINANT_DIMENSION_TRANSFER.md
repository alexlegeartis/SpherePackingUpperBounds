# A dimension-uniform near-frame determinant theorem

## Attribution and novelty correction (2026-11-16)

The determinant step is already contained in Rémi Géraud-Stewart and David Naccache, *A Focus on Hermite Constant 11: the certificates and macadamia 0052* (8 November 2026), Appendix D, Lemma 32, pp. 44–45: https://eprint.iacr.asia/2026/2826.pdf . For n >= 7, the contact inequalities E_ii+E_jj >= 2|E_ij| and s=tr(E) in [0,1] imply det(I+E) >= 1+s-(n-1)s^2/4 when the right side is positive. Substituting E=G/2-I and s=S/2 gives the determinant range used here. Its proof already uses positive/negative-spike diagonals, fixed off-diagonal ratios, and log-determinant concavity. The derivations/checkers below are independent reconstructions, not a novelty claim for this lemma or proof. Any potential novelty must lie elsewhere and needs a separate literature comparison.

Let n≥3 and let Λ⊂R^n have minimum nonzero vector length at least1. Choose representatives v_1,…,v_n of n distinct antipodal pairs in the same class modulo2Λ. Put V=(v_1,…,v_n), G=VᵀV, a_i=|v_i|²−2 and S=Σa_i.

Define

    A_n=8/(n−1),
    B_n=4(n−2)²/(3n²−8n+6),
    δ_n=min(A_n,B_n).

**Theorem.** If S≤δ_n, then V is nonsingular and det(G/2)≥1. Consequently (V/2)D_n is a full-rank sublattice of Λ with covolume at least2^(1−n/2). For every n≥9, δ_n=A_n=8/(n−1). One of the v_i may have squared length below2.

## Proof

For i≠j the vectors (v_i±v_j)/2 are nonzero lattice vectors. Thus a_i+a_j≥0 and, on writing E=G−2I,

    |E_ij|≤(a_i+a_j)/2.

Summing the first inequality shows S≥0. If S=0, all a_i=0 and E=0, proving the result. Assume S>0. At most one a_i is negative.

The polytope P_S={a: Σa_i=S, a_i+a_j≥0} is the convex hull of 2n vectors:

    A_i=S e_i,
    B_i=(S/(n−2))(1,…,1,−1,1,…,1),

where the negative entry in B_i is at i. Indeed a nonnegative a is a convex combination of the A_i. If a_i=−z<0, all other entries are at least z, so S≥(n−2)z. Subtract weight (n−2)z/S of B_i: the residual has all entries nonnegative and total S−(n−2)z, so it is a weighted combination of the A_j.

Fix r_ij=2E_ij/(a_i+a_j) when the denominator is positive, and any value in [−1,1] when it is zero. At fixed r, G(a) has diagonal2+a_i and offdiagonal r_ij(a_i+a_j)/2, hence is affine in a. We verify positive definiteness and det(G/2)≥1 at every vertex. Concavity of logdet on positive definite matrices then proves the assertion throughout P_S.

### Positive spike A_i

Only the i-th offdiagonal row/column can be nonzero. Its entries b_j have |b_j|≤S/2. The Schur complement gives

    det(G/2)=1+S/2−Σ b_j²/4
             ≥1+S/2−(n−1)S²/16.

This is at least1 for S≤A_n. The smallest eigenvalue is at least 2−S(√n−1)/2, which is strictly positive throughout S≤A_n because A_n(√n−1)=8/(√n+1)<4 for n≥3.

### Negative spike B_i

Write z=S/(n−2). Coordinate i decouples, with E_ii=−z. The other (n−1)×(n−1) block has diagonal z and offdiagonal entries of magnitude at most z. Therefore

    ||E||≤(n−1)z,
    tr E=(n−2)z,
    tr E²≤((n−1)²+1)z².

For |x|≤r<1, the power-series bound log(1+x)≥x−x²/[2(1−r)] implies

    log det(G/2) ≥ (n−2)z/2
      − ((n−1)²+1)z² / [8(1−(n−1)z/2)].

This is nonnegative provided

    z≤4(n−2)/(3n²−8n+6),

equivalently S≤B_n. This range also has (n−1)z<2, so G is positive definite. (The inequality is strict since 4(n−2)(n−1)<2(3n²−8n+6) for all n≥3.) This proves both vertex assertions.

Finally, D_n={x∈Z^n:Σx_i even} is generated by e_i±e_j, so (V/2)D_n⊂Λ. Its covolume is 2 det(V)/2^n≥2^(1−n/2).

The inequality A_n≤B_n is equivalent to n³−11n²+24n−16≥0. At n=9 this polynomial is38>0, and its derivative is positive for all real n≥9. Thus A_n is the smaller threshold for every integer n≥9.

## What transfers, and what does not

This is a dimension-uniform geometric determinant theorem, not a dimension-uniform packing-density bound. The finite quotient Λ/((V/2)D_n), its torsion obstructions, and the radial auxiliary-function certificates still depend on n. No dimension other than16 has acquired a new numerical density bound merely from this theorem.
